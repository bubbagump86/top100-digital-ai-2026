How big tech fits in

Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft, Amazon, Alibaba, Baidu and Tencent have enormous AI businesses, but they are not AI companies — their market caps are priced mostly on search, ads, e-commerce and gaming. Using full market caps would let a search business win an AI ranking on non-AI revenue.

So the valuation axis means one thing everywhere: "what is this AI business worth?" Three bases:

  • Pure-play — it is an AI company; its market valuation is already an AI valuation. Used as-is.
  • AI-attributed — diversified, but discloses AI revenue. We value that revenue at the pure-play median EV/Revenue multiple (20×, derived from 81 pure-play companies) to get an implied standalone AI valuation.
  • Proxy — no AI-revenue disclosure at all; full market cap used as a flagged proxy and the ARR axis scored at zero.

Result: Microsoft #3, Alphabet #4, Amazon #5, Meta #6, Alibaba #8, Tencent #10, Baidu #11 — on a like-for-like AI basis. Full working, including the sensitivity of the 20× multiple, is on the methodology page (section 6b).

What this is

AI startup headlines usually mix private valuations with public-market caps, conflate revenue with projections, mix in hardware, and mix in services. This site isolates the digital AI business — foundation models, AI applications, agent platforms, AI coding, voice and video gen, AI-native data, and the AI businesses of the large caps — and ranks them with one transparent scoring rule.

Want to skim the list or sort by ARR/valuation? Go to the full ranking. Care about the math? See methodology. Curious about who got cut? See exclusions.

Is the 20× peer multiple load-bearing?

The multiple used to value the AI businesses of the megacaps is the second-most-consequential number in the model. We stress-tested it five ways: distribution, comparability by scale, geography, a cross-check against what the market actually pays, and a full re-rank from 10× to 40×.

Result: the top 11 is identical at every multiple from 10× to 40×. The number moves the derived dollar values, not the ordering. We also found the one row where the peer multiple overruns the company it is applied to — Baidu, where the uncapped derivation would be 5.4× its entire market cap. A derived valuation can never exceed the company's actual valuation, so Baidu's valuation axis is capped at its $27.4B market cap and the row is badged as indicative. Full write-up in the methodology page, section 6c.

Is the ranking robust?

The log transform is the most consequential choice in the model, so we tested the alternative rather than assuming it. Re-running the full ranking four ways:

The top 7 are identical under every variant — the headline doesn't depend on the scale choice. But raw min-max looks superficially similar (Spearman +0.937) while quietly pushing 87 of 100 rows below 5/100: Anthropic's $100B ARR owns the entire raw axis range, so every company under ~$1B ARR is crushed to zero and “no disclosed revenue” starts winning by default. Full write-up in the methodology page, section 5b.
