Methodology

Everything you need to defend, attack, or reproduce the ranking.

1. The problem

AI startups sit on a four-decade spread: a small enterprise research lab can sit at $1B valuation, $10M ARR while a foundation-model leader sits at $965B valuation, $30B ARR. Both are arguably "top of the AI market", but they are not directly comparable.

A naive ranking that just sums dollars turns this list into "the companies with the most dollars", which is not interesting: Salesforce and Palantir would always win. A ranking that only uses revenue penalises pre-revenue research labs like Safe Superintelligence and Thinking Machines, which are arguably the most strategically important AI companies in the world.

We want a composite that respects scale of capital (valuation) and scale of revenue (ARR), with a normalisation that does not let the biggest player crush everything else.

2. Eligibility: pure-play digital AI

A company must be primarily a digital AI business to be ranked. Concretely, we exclude the entire chip, datacenter hardware, humanoid robotics, autonomous vehicles, and physical-world-AI category:

  • Hardware / silicon: NVIDIA, Cerebras, Groq, Tenstorrent, Rebellions, SambaNova, FuriosaAI, Lightmatter, Etched, Positron, MatX
  • Datacenter / GPU-cloud infra: CoreWeave, Lambda Labs, Crusoe, Applied Digital, Northern Data, Nebius
  • Robotics: Figure, 1X, Apptronik, Sanctuary, Agility, Skild AI, Physical Intelligence, Skydio
  • Autonomous vehicles: Waymo, Cruise, Zoox, Aurora, Tesla autonomy, Mobileye
  • Physical/industrial AI: Anduril, Shield AI, KoBold Metals, Relativity Space, Hadrian

Companies like Databricks, xAI, Palantir, ServiceNow, Salesforce, and C3.ai are included despite also selling adjacent offerings, because their AI segment is the primary value driver and dominant revenue line.

3. The two input axes

For each eligible company we collect:

  1. Valuation — the most recent primary or tender round, public market cap, or transaction valuation. We always prefer a primary-source figure disclosed within the last 18 months. For listed companies we use the full-company valuation — the market is already pricing the AI bet into the total cap.
  2. ARR — AI-attributed ARR only for any company that sells both AI and non-AI products. For pure-AI companies, total ARR equals AI-attributed ARR.

Why AI-attributed ARR matters

Naively summing ARR across a Salesforce ($41.5B total revenue) or ServiceNow ($15.8B total) ranks them above every AI-native competitor, but >90% of that revenue is pre-AI software (CRM, ITSM, etc.) with only an incremental "Agentforce" or "Now Assist" slice attributable to the AI wave. That's not a fair comparison against Anthropic ($30B ARR — all of which is AI). For this edition we use:

  • Salesforce — Agentforce ARR $800M (Q4 FY26 disclosure; +169% YoY). The wider "Agentforce + Data 360" $2.9B figure is partly the acquired Informatica cloud business and doesn't cleanly attribute to AI.
  • ServiceNow — Now Assist + AI portfolio $1.5B FY26 forecast (Q2 2026 actual ACV was $1B). About 10% of total subscription revenue.
  • Palantir — AIP is the primary growth engine but the company does not disclose AIP revenue separately. We mark AI-attributed ARR as null rather than guess; the company scores on valuation only.
  • Samsara — AI-attributed ARR is null. The company sells sensor hardware (cameras, telematics); the AI software layer's ARR is not separately disclosed.
  • Schrodinger — AI-attributed ARR is null. Bunsen (agentic AI co-scientist) launched in mid-2026 in early access with no disclosed ARR; core business ($200M+ ACV) is physics-based molecular simulation, not AI.

Effect on the ranking: Salesforce drops from #3 to #7, ServiceNow from #4 to #5, Palantir from #6 to #25, Samsara from #11 to #48, Schrodinger from #33 to #86. Pure-AI names like Databricks, Cursor, and Mistral rise into the top 10.

4. Why log-scale?

Valuation and ARR each span 5+ orders of magnitude across the eligible set. In a linear scale, Salesforce's $38B ARR is 3,800× Reworkd AI's $10M ARR — a single company becomes ~40% of the entire axis range just from being the largest. The histogram becomes unrecoverable below the giants.

Taking log10 compresses extremes into a much more information-dense scale. After log, Salesforce is at 4.58, Reworkd is at 1.00 — a 4.6× ratio, not 3,800×. This is the standard treatment for financial power-law distributions (Schumpeter, Axtell, etc.). 

log_val = log10(valuation_USD_b)
log_arr = log10(arr_USD_m)

5. Min-max rescaling

We linearly rescale each axis to [0, 1] using the minimum and maximum observed log values. The top observation is 1.0, the bottom 0.0. This is the simplest approach and is enough for this kind of heterogeneous cross-sectional ranking. 

z_log(x) = (log10(x) - log_min) / (log_max - log_min)

For the 2026 ranked set:

  • Valuation: log10 ranges from -1.00 (~$0.1B) to 2.98 (~$965B)
  • ARR: log10 ranges from 1.00 (~$10M) to 4.58 (~$38B)

6. The composite

Composite score is the equal-weighted average of the two rescaled axes, expressed on a 0-100 scale: 

S = 100 * (W_VAL * z_log(val) + W_ARR * z_log(arr))
with W_VAL = W_ARR = 0.5

This is the simplest justifiable weighting that doesn't privilege private-market hype over disclosed revenue (or vice versa). It also makes the score interpretable: a score of 100 means best on both axes; a score of 50 means average on both axes.

Why not a geometric mean?

A geometric mean would punish companies strong on one axis but weak on the other. We deliberately do not do this: pre-revenue labs like SSI and Thinking Machines are strategically important even though their ARR is 0 — a pure valuation signal deserves credit for them. Likewise, high-revenue companies without a recent funding round (e.g. C3.ai, PolyAI, DeepL) are correctly rewarded. The arithmetic mean treats the axes as complements, not substitutes.

Why equal weights?

Sensitivity to the weight choice is a real concern. We tested the ranking with W_VAL = 0.6 / W_ARR = 0.4 (used in some VC-readiness scorecards) and with W_VAL = 0.4 / W_ARR = 0.6 (used in some growth-investing scorecards). The top 10 was stable; reordering happened mostly in the 30-70 range, which is what we'd expect from any composite.

7. Missing data rules

Two kinds of missing data are common in 2026:

  1. Valuation missing, ARR present (e.g. Midjourney, Suno, Aleph Alpha). These are mature, profitable businesses that have no need for outside capital. The valuation axis is set to 0 (worst observed), so they compete on the ARR axis only.
  2. ARR missing, valuation present (e.g. DeepSeek, SSI, Thinking Machines). These are pre-revenue frontier labs whose capital signal is the most important available. The ARR axis is set to 0, and they compete on valuation only.
  3. Both missing. We drop them entirely.

This is a permissive rule. A strict alternative would be to lower missing values to a sentinel below the observed minimum (e.g. log value = log_min - 0.5). We chose 0 because it is interpretable and makes the missing-data penalty visible in the score.

8. Worked example: Anthropic

Anthropic is the #1 company in the 2026 ranking. Inputs: 

valuation_USD_b = 965
arr_USD_m       = 30000

After log10: 

log_val = 2.984 (within [−1.00, 2.984])
log_arr = 4.477 (within [1.00, 4.580])

Rescaled: 

z_val = (2.984 − (−1.00)) / (2.984 − (−1.00)) = 1.000
z_arr = (4.477 − 1.00) / (4.580 − 1.00) = 0.971

Composite: 

S = 100 * (0.5 * 1.000 + 0.5 * 0.971) = 98.57 / 100

Sits at the top because (a) its valuation is at the upper edge of the observed range, and (b) its ARR is the highest of any private company. OpenAI is #2 with very similar numbers (1.0 valuation normalised, 0.939 ARR normalised → 96.78).

10. Limitations

  • Stale numbers. Private-market round prices change fast. We use the latest figure we could verify through October 2026.
  • Annualised vs. real. Many companies publish "run-rate ARR" rather than booked ARR. We accept run-rate but flag it in the source column.
  • AI-attribution is partial. For the listed traditional-tech firms we used the AI-attributed disclosure where it exists (Salesforce Agentforce, ServiceNow Now Assist). For firms that don't break it out (Palantir, Samsara, Schrodinger) we mark AI-attributed ARR as null. This is the conservative choice — it under-counts real AI revenue at those firms, but it keeps the ranking defensible.
  • Valuation is full-company for listed names. We don't try to subtract a "non-AI" slice of the market cap. The market is already pricing the AI bet into the total cap, and any "AI-only market cap" estimate would require assumptions we can't defend.
  • Category boundaries. Some companies straddle categories (Databricks = data + AI; Scale AI = data labeling + defense). They sit where they sit on company self-description.
  • Public-company revenue cycle. The latest Salesforce / ServiceNow / Palantir figures all come from FY2026 filings dated Feb 2026 – Aug 2026. Quarter-end number will move on the next earnings cycle.
