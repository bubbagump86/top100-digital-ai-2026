Methodology

Everything you need to defend, attack, or reproduce the ranking.

1. The problem

AI startups sit on a four-decade spread: a small enterprise research lab can sit at $1B valuation, $10M ARR while a foundation-model leader sits at $965B valuation, $100B ARR. Both are arguably "top of the AI market", but they are not directly comparable.

A naive ranking that just sums dollars turns this list into "the companies with the most dollars", which is not interesting: a $4T megacap would always win. A ranking that only uses revenue penalises pre-revenue research labs like Safe Superintelligence and Thinking Machines, which are arguably the most strategically important AI companies in the world.

We want a composite that respects scale of capital (valuation) and scale of revenue (ARR), with a normalisation that does not let the biggest player crush everything else — and that can hold both a pure-play AI lab and a $4 trillion megacap to the same standard.

2. Eligibility: digital AI

A company must be primarily a digital AI business to be ranked. Concretely, we exclude the entire chip, datacenter hardware, humanoid robotics, autonomous vehicles, and physical-world-AI category:

  • Hardware / silicon: NVIDIA, Cerebras, Groq, Tenstorrent, Rebellions, SambaNova, FuriosaAI, Lightmatter, Etched, Positron, MatX, TSMC, Broadcom
  • Datacenter / GPU-cloud infra: CoreWeave, Lambda Labs, Crusoe, Applied Digital, Northern Data, Nebius
  • Robotics: Figure, 1X, Apptronik, Sanctuary, Agility, Skild AI, Physical Intelligence, Skydio
  • Autonomous vehicles: Waymo, Cruise, Zoox, Aurora, Tesla, Mobileye
  • Physical/industrial AI: Anduril, Shield AI, KoBold Metals, Relativity Space, Hadrian
  • No AI revenue line at all: Apple (Apple Intelligence is not separately monetised; a $4.95T market cap would make the valuation axis a measure of consumer-electronics franchises, not AI)

3. The two input axes

For each eligible company we collect:

  1. Valuation — the most recent primary or tender round, public market cap, or transaction valuation, expressed on a like-for-like AI basis (see section 6b).
  2. AI-attributed ARR — for any company that sells both AI and non-AI products, we take only the AI-attributed revenue. For pure-AI companies, total ARR equals AI-attributed ARR.

Why AI-attributed ARR matters

Summing ARR across a Salesforce ($41.5B total revenue) or ServiceNow ($15.8B total) ranks them above every AI-native competitor, but >90% of that revenue is pre-AI software (CRM, ITSM) with only an incremental "Agentforce" or "Now Assist" slice attributable to the AI wave. That is not a fair comparison against Anthropic ($100B ARR — all of it AI).

Our AI-attribution policy for every company that sells AI alongside non-AI software:

  • Microsoft — $37B AI business ARR (Q3 FY26, +123% YoY). The most transparently disclosed AI revenue figure of any diversified company in this list.
  • Amazon (AWS AI) — $25B+ annual run rate (Q2 2026, triple-digit YoY growth). Excludes the separate silicon/chips business.
  • Google — ~$30B estimate. Alphabet discloses no standalone Gemini revenue, so we estimate the AI-attributable share of the $99B Google Cloud run-rate plus Gemini API and consumer AI subscriptions. Flagged as an estimate.
  • Meta — $20B+ Advantage+ AI advertising campaign run-rate. The Meta AI chatbot and Llama/Muse API generate $0 direct disclosed revenue.
  • Alibaba — $9.3B (AI-related product revenue $1.824B/quarter annualised, plus the AI Labs and Applications segment). Both lines are disclosed.
  • Baidu — $7.4B from its own "Core AI-Powered Business" segment — the most AI-concentrated disclosure of any company in this list (>52% of core revenue).
  • Tencent — ~$7B estimate, from ~40% of Tencent Cloud revenue. Tencent discloses no AI revenue line. Weakest of the big-tech AI figures here.
  • Salesforce — Agentforce ARR $800M (Q4 FY26, +169% YoY). The wider "Agentforce + Data 360" $2.9B figure is partly the acquired Informatica cloud business and doesn't cleanly attribute to AI.
  • ServiceNow — Now Assist + AI portfolio $1.5B FY26 forecast (Q2 2026 actual ACV: $1B). About 10% of total subscription revenue.
  • Notion AI — $300M AI revenue slice (estimate).
  • Palantir, Samsara, Schrodinger, ByteDance, Cohesity, Talkdesk — no AI-attributed revenue disclosed. Handled as market-cap proxies (section 6b).

4. Why log-scale?

Valuation and ARR each span 5+ orders of magnitude across the eligible set. In a linear scale, Anthropic's $100B ARR is 10,000× Reworkd AI's $10M ARR — a single company becomes ~40% of the entire axis range just from being the largest. The histogram becomes unrecoverable below the giants.

Taking log10 compresses extremes into a much more information-dense scale. After log, Anthropic is at 5.00, Reworkd is at 1.00 — a 5× ratio, not 10,000×. This is the standard treatment for financial power-law distributions. 

log_val = log10(valuation_USD_b)
log_arr = log10(arr_USD_m)

5. Min-max rescaling

We linearly rescale each axis to [0, 1] using the minimum and maximum observed log values. The top observation is 1.0, the bottom 0.0. This is the simplest approach and is enough for this kind of heterogeneous cross-sectional ranking. 

z_log(x) = (log10(x) - log_min) / (log_max - log_min)

For the 2026 ranked set:

  • Valuation: log10 ranges from -1.00 (~$0.1B) to 2.98 (~$965B)
  • ARR: log10 ranges from 1.00 (~$10M) to 5.00 (~$100B)

6. The composite

Composite score is the equal-weighted average of the two rescaled axes, expressed on a 0-100 scale: 

S = 100 * (W_VAL * z_log(val) + W_ARR * z_log(arr))
with W_VAL = W_ARR = 0.5

This is the simplest justifiable weighting that doesn't privilege private-market hype over disclosed revenue (or vice versa). It also makes the score interpretable: a score of 100 means best on both axes; a score of 50 means average on both axes.

Why not a geometric mean?

A geometric mean would punish companies strong on one axis but weak on the other. We deliberately do not do this: pre-revenue labs like SSI and Thinking Machines are strategically important even though their ARR is 0 — a pure valuation signal deserves credit for them. Likewise, high-revenue companies without a recent funding round (e.g. C3.ai, PolyAI, DeepL) are correctly rewarded. The arithmetic mean treats the axes as complements, not substitutes.

Why equal weights?

Sensitivity to the weight choice is a real concern. We tested the ranking with W_VAL = 0.6 / W_ARR = 0.4 (used in some VC-readiness scorecards) and with W_VAL = 0.4 / W_ARR = 0.6 (used in some growth-investing scorecards). The top 10 was stable; reordering happened mostly in the 30-70 range, which is what we'd expect from any composite.

6b. Putting big tech in the list — the three-basis model

This is the hardest problem in the ranking, and the part most likely to be done badly.

Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft, Amazon, Alibaba, Baidu and Tencent have enormous, real, disclosed AI businesses. Excluding them would understate the most important AI businesses on earth. But using their full market caps would let a search-ad business or an e-commerce business win an AI ranking on the strength of non-AI revenue — which is exactly the error we corrected for Salesforce and ServiceNow.

We resolve it by making the valuation axis mean one thing everywhere: "what is this AI business worth?" Three bases:

Basis 1 — Pure-play (market-set AI valuation)

The company is an AI business. Its market or transaction valuation is already an AI valuation, so we use it as-is. Anthropic's $965B is the market's price for Anthropic-the-AI-company. No adjustment.

Basis 2 — AI-attributed (derived)

The company is diversified, but discloses AI-attributed revenue. We take that revenue and value it at the pure-play median EV/Revenue multiple, producing an implied "what would this AI business be worth as a standalone AI company?" figure. 

implied AI valuation = AI-attributed ARR x peer median EV/Revenue

The multiple is computed from the pure-play pool only — so the imputation never feeds back into its own input — and then applied once. For the 2026 set:

  • Pool: 81 pure-play companies with both a valuation and an ARR figure
  • Median multiple: 20.0×
  • Interquartile range: 15× – 40×
  • Range: 5× (Mercor, Otter.ai) to 324× (Zhipu AI)

Worked examples:

Microsoft   $37,000M AI ARR x 20x = $740B implied AI valuation
Google      $30,000M AI ARR x 20x = $600B implied AI valuation
Amazon      $25,000M AI ARR x 20x = $500B implied AI valuation
Meta        $20,000M AI ARR x 20x = $400B implied AI valuation
Alibaba      $9,300M AI ARR x 20x = $186B implied AI valuation
Baidu        $7,440M AI ARR x 20x = $149B implied AI valuation
Tencent      $7,000M AI ARR x 20x = $140B implied AI valuation
ServiceNow   $1,500M AI ARR x 20x =  $30B implied AI valuation
Salesforce     $800M AI ARR x 20x =  $16B implied AI valuation

Compare those to the full-company market caps: Microsoft $3.95T, Alphabet $4.16T, Amazon $2.83T, Meta $1.77T, Tencent $471B, Alibaba $270B, Baidu $27.4B, Salesforce $280B, ServiceNow $200B. The gap between each pair is the part of the company that is not AI — and it is usually 95%+ of the market cap. Salesforce's implied AI valuation of $16B against a $280B market cap is the starkest example: Agentforce is real, and it is also ~6% of what the market pays for Salesforce.

Basis 3 — Market-cap proxy

The company discloses no AI-attributed revenue, so no AI valuation can be derived. We use the full market cap as a flagged proxy and score the ARR axis at zero. This simultaneously overstates its AI valuation and understates its AI revenue, so these rows are the weakest evidence in the list and are badged accordingly. Applies to Palantir, Samsara, Schrodinger, ByteDance, Cohesity and Talkdesk.

What this changes

Big tech lands in the top 11 on a like-for-like AI basis — not because search ads are AI, but because once you strip the non-AI business out and value the remainder at peer AI multiples, their AI businesses genuinely are that large. Anthropic and OpenAI remain #1 and #2 because their AI is the whole company, and their AI is worth more per dollar of revenue than anyone else's.

7. Missing data rules

Two kinds of missing data are common in 2026:

  1. Valuation missing, ARR present (e.g. Midjourney, Suno, Aleph Alpha). These are mature, profitable businesses that have no need for outside capital. The valuation axis is set to 0 (worst observed), so they compete on the ARR axis only.
  2. ARR missing, valuation present (e.g. DeepSeek, SSI, Thinking Machines, Magic AI). These are pre-revenue frontier labs whose capital signal is the most important available. The ARR axis is set to 0, and they compete on valuation only.
  3. Both missing. We drop them entirely.

This is a permissive rule. A strict alternative would be to lower missing values to a sentinel below the observed minimum. We chose 0 because it is interpretable and makes the missing-data penalty visible in the score.

8. Worked example: Anthropic

Anthropic is the #1 company in the 2026 ranking. Inputs:

valuation_USD_b = 965
arr_USD_m       = 100000   (> $100B annualised run-rate, mid-Sep 2026)

After log10:

log_val = 2.985   (observed max = 2.985)
log_arr = 5.000   (observed max = 5.000)

Rescaled:

z_val = (2.985 − (−1.00)) / (2.985 − (−1.00)) = 1.000
z_arr = (5.000 − 1.00) / (5.000 − 1.00) = 1.000

Composite:

S = 100 * (0.5 * 1.000 + 0.5 * 1.000) = 100.00 / 100

Anthropic sits at the top because it maxes both axes: the highest valuation in the set and the highest AI-attributed ARR in the set. OpenAI is #2 (z_val 0.987, z_arr 0.912 → 95.56).

And a derived example: Microsoft

AI ARR                  = $37,000M  (disclosed, Q3 FY26)
peer median multiple     = 20.0x     (n=81 pure-play companies)
implied AI valuation     = $37,000M x 20.0x = $740B
log_val = log10(740) = 2.869
z_val   = (2.869 − (−1.00)) / 3.985 = 0.971
log_arr = log10(37000) = 4.568
z_arr   = (4.568 − 1.00) / 4.00 = 0.892
S       = 100 * (0.5 * 0.971 + 0.5 * 0.892) = 93.16  → rank #3

The three-basis model is implemented in scripts/score.py: the peer multiple is derived in pass 1 from the pure-play pool only, derived valuations are resolved in pass 2, and the axes are normalised and scored in pass 3.

10. Limitations

  • Stale numbers. Private-market round prices change fast. We use the latest figure we could verify through October 2026.
  • Annualised vs. real. Many companies publish "run-rate ARR" rather than booked ARR. We accept run-rate but flag it in the source column.
  • Two of the big-tech AI figures are estimates, not disclosures. Google (~$30B) and Tencent (~$7B) do not publish AI revenue. Their rows are flagged estimated in the source column. Every other big-tech AI figure is a company disclosure.
  • The peer multiple is a single number. 20× is a median across 81 companies spanning $10M to $100B ARR. A 15× multiple would put Microsoft at #3 with a $555B implied valuation instead of $740B, and would not change the identity of the top 3. A 40× multiple (the p75) would lift the big-tech rows further.
  • Derived valuations are not market prices. A "derived" valuation of $740B for Microsoft's AI business is an analytical construct, not something you can sell. It answers "what would this be worth at peer AI multiples", not "what is it worth today".
  • Proxy rows are weak. Palantir, Samsara, Schrodinger, ByteDance, Cohesity and Talkdesk disclose no AI-attributed revenue and are scored on full market cap with a zero ARR axis. Treat their ranks as indicative only.
  • Category boundaries. Some companies straddle categories (Databricks = data + AI; Scale AI = data labeling + defense). They sit where they sit on company self-description.
  • Recent M&A. Several big movers in 2025-2026 are now subsidiaries of larger companies, and their valuations are the last reported private round before the deal closed.
    • SpaceXAI (xAI + Cursor + X) — xAI acquired by SpaceX all-stock for $250B, closed Feb 2, 2026; Cursor acquired all-stock for $60B, signed Jun 16, 2026 and closed Aug 14, 2026. Presented as one consolidated entity with a combined $310B valuation (the sum of the two disclosed transaction prices) and ~$14.2B ARR.
    • Codeium / Windsurf — acquired by Cognition, undisclosed (~$250M est.), closed Jul 14, 2025 with $82M ARR. Dropped as a standalone row because its ARR is now inside Cognition's consolidated figure.
    • Hugging Face — acquired by NVIDIA in 2025; its $12.93B is the acquisition price, which remains a valid AI valuation.
